# The neutrino sector adds nothing, and a ceiling falls out

The test was designed to falsify the measure: if reach is a real property of a unified model, adding
the neutrino sector — where SO(10) and Pati-Salam are supposed to shine — should raise it. It does
not.

## Type-I seesaw

M_nu = -M_D^T M_R^{-1} M_D. In SO(10) the Dirac matrix comes from the same 10_H contraction as
M_u, so M_D is proportional to M_u: a genuine quark-lepton link. But M_R is a free symmetric 3x3,
and the map M_R to M_nu is surjective onto symmetric matrices. For any light-neutrino spectrum there
is an M_R reproducing it, so no relation survives. The link constrains M_D, which is not observable.

## Type-II

M_nu proportional to Y_126, while M_e = Y_10 v_d - 3 Y_126 v_d'. An exact proportionality would need
Y_10 = 0, which also sends M_u to zero. Again nothing.

A relation appears only if one *declares* type-II dominance or M_R proportional to the identity.
That declaration is an input, not a consequence of the group. The sector in which unified models are
advertised as most predictive is the sector where the predictivity is bought.

## The ceiling

**Theorem.** Let a model be spectrum-viable: its charged-fermion matrices reproduce m_d != m_e and
m_b != m_tau. Then the number of exact relations is three, independent of the group.

Proof: a visible relation needs a scalar proportionality between two sectors; that holds only if one
invariant contraction feeds both; splitting M_d from M_e requires at least two independent
contractions; hence no mass relation survives, and what remains is gauge unification (2) and charge
quantisation (1).

## Correction

An earlier version of the comparison table mixed exact and approximate relations. Separated, the
viable models carry three exact relations and at most one approximate one.
